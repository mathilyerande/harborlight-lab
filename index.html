The Harborlight Income Statement Lab

Follow one small business through its income statement. Build it a line at a time, change the numbers and watch the bottom line move, then work out why a growing business made almost no profit the next year.

The company. Harborlight Coffee Roasters is a fictional café and roastery. It serves coffee by the cup and sells roasted beans by the bag. We follow its income statement for one month, June.
The question. After a month of selling, how much is left for the owners, and what decides it? (All figures are made up for teaching.)

1. Build the income statement, line by line

An income statement answers one question: did the business make money over a period of time? Start at the top with sales and subtract costs until you reach the bottom line. Press Next line to reveal each one, or click a line to read about it again.

    Move the sliders. Everything below recalculates. Watch which lines move and which stay put: rent does not care how many cups you sell, but cost of goods sold does.

    Challenges

    3. Sales grew. So why did profit collapse?

    A year later, Harborlight has opened a second cart, run a promotion on cups, hired staff and borrowed more. Compare the same month, June, in both years, then take a position before the answer is shown.

    Right-hand columns show each line as a share of that year's revenue (a "common-size" view).

    4. Check your understanding

    Harborlight Coffee Roasters is a fictional company and every number here is invented for teaching. All amounts are for one month. The model is simplified: one tax rate, no inventory change, depreciation is straight-line. Nothing you enter leaves your device.
